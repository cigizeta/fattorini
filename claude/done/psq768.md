# psq768: does psq's castling and en passant pay off?

**Status: done and retired.** Drawn against psq on 2026-10-19 (906 games, S 0.4989, TODO.md 1b),
so psq keeps its 780 rows; psq768's code removed on 2026-10-20 (psq768-removal.md), tag 10 left
refused by name.

Status as it was: implemented 2026-10-18; `cargo test -p smithy --release` passing on the training machine the
same day. Next: forge `lc0-psq768` beside `lc0-psq`. The experiments run on Lc0's labels only (the developer,
2026-10-18); self-play trains only the best `lc0-` shape afterwards.

## Question

Psq is bullet's Chess768 plus 4 castling rows and 8 en passant file rows, 780 in all. Do those 12
rows earn their keep? They cost the engine accumulator updates on every move that changes castling
rights or the en passant file, and they may or may not help the evaluation. psq768 is psq without
them, a true 768-row network end to end, so the A/B measures both sides: the Elo the rows bring
and the speed they cost.

Configs: `lc0-psq768.yaml` and `self-psq768.yaml`, lc0-psq's and self-psq's field for field except
`kind: psq768`.

## Design

**The developer's decisions (2026-10-18):**

- The network must really be 768 rows wide, in training and in the engine, or the speed-up is not
  tested. Zeroing 12 rows of a 780-row file was rejected.
- psq768 is its own variant everywhere (config kind, feature set, engine tag), not a flag on psq:
  simpler, and if the experiment goes as expected psq768 becomes the new psq, so the variant is
  what survives and psq's 12 rows are what gets deleted.

Only plain psq gets a 768 twin: psq_threats numbers its threat rows from `psq::ROWS`, and the
pairwise and stack kinds are out of scope until psq768 has won.

### Config (`net_config.rs`)

`ArchitectureKind::Psq768`, spelled `psq768` in YAML (`#[serde(rename = "psq768")]`, since
`snake_case` would not produce it). Same fields as `Psq`. The experiments test lists the two configs
and maps `psq768` to the new variant.

### Features (`rotor::features::psq768`)

A module beside `psq`: `ROWS = 768`, `active_rows` and `expand` returning psq's piece rows only.
It reuses `psq::piece_row`, so the two agree on every piece row by construction and psq768's row
`r` means exactly psq's row `r`.

### Training (smithy)

- `FeatureSet::Psq768`, mapped from `ArchitectureKind::Psq768`.
- `batch.rs`: `DenseBatch::Psq768(DenseBatchPsq)`, built like psq's batch with `psq768::ROWS` and
  `psq768::expand`.
- `nnue_network.rs`: `NetworkPsq::with_rows(vs, psq768::ROWS, false, false, ...)`; every
  `FeatureSet::Psq => psq::ROWS` site gains its `Psq768 => psq768::ROWS` arm.
- `export.rs`: writes `psq768::ROWS` transform rows under the new tag.
- Everything matching on `FeatureSet`/`ArchitectureKind` gets the arm the compiler asks for, and
  nothing more.

### Engine (`rotor::nnue`)

- New tag `FEATURE_SET_PSQ768 = 10`, name `"psq768"`, `shape_of` -> `Some((psq768::ROWS, 2))`. A tag
  of its own for the reason `FEATURE_SET_PSQ_PAIRWISE` gives: an older build refuses the file.
- `PsqNetwork` and its quantized twin gain `castling_en_passant: bool`, as they have `threats` and
  `pairwise` (an internal field, set from the tag; the file and config know only `psq768`).
  `rows()` returns `psq768::ROWS` when false.
- Full refresh (the `psq::active_rows` calls around lines 306, 763, 784) uses `psq768::active_rows`
  when false.
- Incremental update (around line 1124): the castling and en passant blocks are skipped entirely
  when false -- this is the speed-up being measured.
- `nnue_bench.rs`: a `--psq768` option, so the speed difference is measured directly.

### Tests

- features: psq768's rows are psq's minus every row `>= 768`, for the existing FEN set.
- export round trip for a psq768 network, as for psq.
- nnue: incremental equals full refresh for psq768 through castling and en passant moves; an
  older-tag check that psq768 files are refused under any other tag.
- net_config: `kind: psq768` parses; the experiments test lists the two configs.

## Measurement

1. Train both configs like their psq twins (`smithy forge`).
2. Speed: `nnue_bench` psq vs psq768 on one idle machine, both on the same one.
3. Strength: each gate is against the hand-crafted evaluation, too indirect for a 12-row question.
   A direct arena match of self-psq's winner against self-psq768's, and lc0's likewise, decides.

## Decision rule (the developer, 2026-10-18)

- **Speed does not decide.** On the training machine, idle and loaded, the incremental update differed by less
  than the run-to-run noise (psq 34.2 to 37.7 ns, psq768 35.8 to 38.6 ns, 2026-10-18): the 12 rows
  change on too few moves to show.
- **About equal strength: psq (780) stays.** The rows cost nothing measurable, and the NNUE MCTS
  experiment (`mcts-nnue.md`) may need them: a leaf there is evaluated once with no quiescence,
  and the policy head must tell positions with castling rights from those without (Lc0 feeds its
  network castling rights, and en passant through its history).
- **psq768 clearly stronger:** it becomes psq for alpha-beta, as below, but the MCTS network
  still tests castling and en passant for itself rather than inheriting 768.

## If psq768 wins

psq768 becomes psq: psq's castling and en passant rows, their incremental updates and tag 3's
780-row layout are retired (tag 3 left unused, as tags 0 and 4 were), and the threats, pairwise and
stack kinds rebase on 768 rows. A separate plan.
